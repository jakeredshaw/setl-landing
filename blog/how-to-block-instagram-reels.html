How to block Instagram Reels on iPhone

The honest answer first: iOS cannot block one feature inside an app. Here is what actually reduces Reels, in order of how well it works.

By Jacob Redshaw · 14 September 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer

You cannot block Instagram Reels on their own with iPhone Screen Time, because it blocks whole apps rather than features inside them. The practical options are blocking Instagram at set hours, using it in a browser instead of the app, or deleting the app for a fortnight.

If you want to block instagram reels and keep the rest of Instagram, iPhone cannot do that, and no third party app can either.

Why you cannot block Instagram Reels on their own

Apple's Screen Time framework operates on apps and categories. It can make Instagram unavailable. It has no mechanism to reach inside Instagram and switch off one tab.

Third party blockers run on the same framework and inherit the same limit. Apple deliberately hands blockers opaque tokens representing your chosen apps, so the blocker never learns which apps they are. The privacy protection and the feature limitation are the same mechanism, which is why this will not be fixed.

The only party who could remove Reels cleanly is Meta, and Meta has no commercial reason to build that switch. Short form is where the engagement is.

What actually works, in order

1. Block Instagram for set hours

Specific hours, decided in advance, usually the evening. This is the strongest option because it is enforced and requires nothing of you in the moment. Set a Screen Time Schedule and check Instagram is not in your Always Allowed list.

You lose messages and the rest of the app during those hours too. That is the trade, and the hours you lose to Reels are rarely the hours you would have spent messaging someone important.

2. Use Instagram in Safari rather than the app

Delete the app and use the website. The web version is less comfortable and pushes short form less aggressively, and the discomfort is the point. You do the thing you came for, then stop.

3. Stop engaging with Reels you are served

Recommendation systems respond to behaviour. Not watching, not lingering and not liking does shift what you are shown over weeks. Slow, partial, and genuinely something.

4. Set a Time Allowance on the Social category

A ceiling rather than a block. When it runs out you are told and may continue, which is the weakness. It is good for finding out how much Reels is really costing you, which most people underestimate heavily.

5. Delete the app for two weeks

Best supported by evidence. A randomised study of 2,844 Facebook users found four weeks of verified deactivation produced small but significant wellbeing gains, more offline socialising, and a large persistent drop in use even after the study ended.1

Delete the app, not the account. You keep your messages and photos, and the habit is attached to the icon.

Why short form is harder than the rest of the app

Scrolling a normal feed still involves posts by people you chose to follow, and you eventually reach ones you have seen. There is a vague end.

Reels has no end and no relationship to anyone you know. Each item is seconds long and the next begins before you have evaluated the last, so you never reach a boundary where continuing becomes a decision. You do not choose to watch for forty minutes. You simply never arrive at the point where you would have stopped.

The compression also hides the time. Forty minutes of a film is one memory. Forty minutes of Reels is a hundred items you cannot describe tomorrow, which is why people guess low. Check the number in Settings rather than trusting your sense of it.

The messages problem, which is the real objection

The reason people hesitate to block Instagram is rarely the feed. It is direct messages, because for a lot of people that is where friends actually live, and losing it for an evening has a social cost that a feed does not.

Two honest options. Either accept the trade for a narrow window, usually an hour or two at night, when nobody reasonably expects a reply anyway. Or move the conversations you care about to a messaging app and leave Instagram as the thing it has become for you, which is a video channel.

The second option is more work and it is the one that actually resolves the problem, because it separates the part you value from the part costing you evenings. Most people never consider it, because the app presents both as one inseparable thing, which is a design decision rather than a technical necessity.

What the evidence says about cutting back

A 2025 randomised controlled trial in BMC Medicine took 111 students averaging 276 minutes of phone a day and had 58 hold under two hours a day for three weeks. Against the control they improved on stress, wellbeing, depressive symptoms and sleep quality.2

Screen time then returned towards baseline once the limit ended.2 Whatever you pick, make it permanent rather than a fortnight of effort.

Which rules out the approach most people try first, and the one that feels most virtuous: a hard week off, taken as a kind of penance, after which you expect to return as someone who uses it sensibly. The trial suggests you return as the person you were. Not because the week was wasted, but because nothing about the app or your environment changed while you were away. Shortest advice: you cannot block Reels alone, so block Instagram for the hours you keep losing and use the website for everything else.

Block instagram reels: FAQs

Can you block Instagram Reels without blocking Instagram?

Not on iPhone. Screen Time works on whole apps and categories, so it cannot disable one tab inside an app. Third party blockers use the same framework and have the same limit.

How do I stop seeing Reels?

Block Instagram for set hours, use the website instead of the app, stop engaging with the Reels you are served, or delete the app for a couple of weeks.

Does using Instagram in a browser help?

Often yes. The web version is less comfortable and pushes short form less hard, and making something slightly worse to use is a legitimate strategy rather than a trick.

Why is Reels harder to stop than normal scrolling?

Because each item is seconds long and the next starts before you have evaluated the last, so you never reach a boundary where continuing becomes a conscious decision.

Will not liking Reels reduce how many I see?

Partly and slowly. Recommendations respond to behaviour, so it shifts over weeks, but it is a weak lever compared with blocking the app for specific hours.

Should I delete Instagram instead?

The evidence for deletion is stronger than for limiting. A randomised deactivation study found a lasting drop in use afterwards, while a limiting trial saw usage return to baseline.
