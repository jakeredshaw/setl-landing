one sec alternatives

one sec is excellent at what it does. If the pause has stopped working on you, the thing you need next is a different category of tool.

By Jacob Redshaw · 14 September 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer

The best one sec alternatives depend on why the pause stopped working. ScreenZen is the closest free equivalent, Opal and SETL replace the pause with an actual block, and Jomo suits people who want detailed control. one sec itself remains the gentlest option.

People look for one sec alternatives for one of two reasons, and they lead to opposite answers. Either the pause has stopped working, or you want the same idea somewhere else.

Worth saying first: one sec is a good app. On the UK App Store on 2 November 2026 it held 4.8 stars from 7,133 ratings and was free to download, from riedel.wtf apps.1 That is one of the strongest reputations in the category.

What one sec does, and why it eventually fails for some people

one sec intercepts the app you opened, makes you pause for a moment, then asks whether you still want it.

That works because it targets the reflex rather than the decision. Most app opening is not chosen, it is automatic, and inserting a gap converts an automatic act into a conscious one. For a lot of people that gap is enough and they need nothing else.

Where it stops working is when the opening stops being reflexive. Once you are deliberately going to the app at midnight, knowing exactly what you are doing and intending to do it anyway, a pause is simply a delay you are happy to sit through. The tool has not broken. Your problem has changed shape.

If that is where you are, the category you need is enforcement rather than friction.

It is worth separating those two words, because the whole market blurs them. Friction makes an action slightly harder and leaves the choice with you. Enforcement removes the choice for a defined period. Almost every app in this category sits somewhere on that line, and the mistake people make is assuming that more friction eventually becomes enforcement. It does not. Ten seconds of pause and thirty seconds of pause are the same kind of thing, and neither is the same kind of thing as the app being gone until seven in the morning.

one sec alternatives, by what you actually need

If you want the same idea, free: ScreenZen

Closest equivalent. ScreenZen from ScreenZen LLC was free to download on the UK store on 2 November 2026 with 4.8 stars from 7,588 ratings.2 It works through pauses, intention prompts and limits on how many times you can open something.

More configurable than one sec, which is both the appeal and the catch, since a tool you can reconfigure can be reconfigured at the worst moment.

If the pause is not enough: Opal

The most established paid option. Opal: Screen Time Control from Opal OS Corporation held 4.6 stars from 14,776 UK ratings on 2 November 2026, by far the most reviewed app in the category.3 Sessions, scheduled blocks and a harder focus mode. The safest buy if review history reassures you, and the most expensive mainstream choice.

If you want detailed control: Jomo

Jomo held 4.8 stars from 471 UK ratings on 2 November 2026 and goes deepest on routines, schedules and rules.4 Good if you enjoy configuring things. A real risk if you do not, because a blocker you have not finished setting up blocks nothing.

If your problem is specifically bedtime: SETL

Mine, so discount accordingly. It blocks automatically at your bedtime rather than waiting for you to open something, which is the main structural difference from one sec: there is no interception because there is nothing to intercept. Ending a block early is a deliberate press and hold rather than a tap. It has no reviews yet, having launched in late October 2026, so if a long track record matters to you, buy Opal.

If you have not tried the free built in option: Apple Screen Time

Set a Screen Time Schedule, empty your Always Allowed list, and use a passcode you have not memorised. For a decent number of people that is the end of the problem and it costs nothing. See how to set one up.

One thing worth knowing before you switch

Changing tools is often a way of feeling like you are addressing something without addressing it. The new app has a novelty period, it works for ten days, and then you are where you were with a different icon.

Evidence worth holding in mind: a 2025 randomised controlled trial in BMC Medicine found real improvements in stress, wellbeing, depressive symptoms and sleep when 58 students held under two hours a day for three weeks, and then found screen time climbing back towards baseline once the arrangement ended.5

So before switching, check whether you actually configured one sec properly and gave it more than a fortnight. The best tool is the one still running in six months, and that is usually a question about setup rather than about which app you chose.

One sec alternatives: FAQs

What is the best alternative to one sec?

It depends why it stopped working. ScreenZen is the closest free equivalent, Opal is the most established paid block, and a scheduled blocker suits you if the problem is specifically late nights.

Is ScreenZen better than one sec?

Not better, more configurable. Both were free to download on the UK store in November 2026 with 4.8 star ratings. ScreenZen gives you more settings, which helps some people and hinders others.

Why has one sec stopped working for me?

Usually because your app opening is no longer reflexive. A pause converts an automatic act into a conscious one, but if you are deliberately going to the app anyway, the pause is just a delay you sit through.

Is there a free alternative to one sec?

Apple Screen Time is free and built in, and ScreenZen is free to download. Set up a Screen Time Schedule properly before paying for anything, because for many people it is enough.

Does one sec block apps completely?

No, and it is not meant to. It inserts a pause and then asks whether you still want the app. If you need the app genuinely unavailable, you want a blocker rather than a friction tool.

Should I switch apps or stick with what I have?

Check first whether you configured your current app properly and gave it more than two weeks. Switching tools often feels like progress while changing nothing but the icon.
