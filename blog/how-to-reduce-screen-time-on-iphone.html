How to reduce screen time on iPhone: 8 practical methods

Cutting phone use is easier when the phone does some of the work. Eight methods, from two minute settings to firmer blocks, each with what the evidence says.

By Jacob Redshaw · 14 September 2026 · 5 min read

Quick answer

To reduce screen time on iPhone, start with your Screen Time report to see where the hours go. Then set app limits or time allowances, batch notifications with Scheduled Summary, try the Grayscale colour filter, use a Focus, tidy your Home Screen, charge your phone outside the bedroom, and use an app blocker for the times you slip.

If you want to reduce screen time, you are in good company. UK adults now spend an average of four and a half hours online a day, and most of that time is on a smartphone, according to Ofcom.1

Relying on willpower alone is hard. The methods below change your phone so the easy choice is the better one. A screen time restrictor is any setting or app that limits when or how long you can use apps, and several of these are built into your iPhone already.

How to reduce screen time on iPhone: 8 methods

Work through them in order. The first few take two minutes; the later ones do more of the heavy lifting.

1. Read your Screen Time report first

You cannot cut what you have not measured. Your screen time report shows the time you spend on your devices, your most used apps, how often you pick up your iPhone, and how many notifications you get.2

  • iOS 27: Settings, then Screen Time, then Daily Average. Tap Week or Day.2
  • iOS 26: Settings, then Screen Time, then See All App & Website Activity.3

Pick the one or two apps that take the most time. Those are your targets, not your whole phone.

2. Set app limits or time allowances

App limits cap how long you can use an app or category each day.

  • iOS 26: Settings, then Screen Time, then App Limits, then Add Limit. Choose categories or apps, tap Next, and set the time.4
  • iOS 27: Settings, then Screen Time, then Time Allowances. Set allowances for categories like Entertainment, Games and Social Media.5

One warning. On iOS 26, Apple says Screen Time limits can be ignored by default once reached.4 Our guide to Screen Time limits not working shows how to make them stick.

3. Cut and batch your notifications

Every buzz is an invitation to pick up the phone. Turn off notifications you do not need in Settings, then Notifications, then tap an app and turn off Allow Notifications.6

For the rest, use Scheduled Summary: Settings, then Notifications, then Scheduled Summary. You choose the times and which apps to include.6

There is research behind batching. In a two week randomised experiment with 237 Android users in India, people whose notifications arrived in batches three times a day felt more attentive, more productive, in a better mood and more in control of their phones. Those who got no notifications at all reported higher anxiety and fear of missing out.7 So batch rather than switch everything off.

4. Try grayscale

Grayscale drains the colour from your screen, which can make apps feel less appealing. Go to Settings, then Accessibility, then Display & Text Size, tap Color Filters, turn them on and pick the grayscale filter.8

A 2020 study of undergraduate students found those who switched to grayscale cut their screen time by an average of 37.9 minutes a day, as summarised in a later study of pharmacy students. In that later study, where grayscale was one of several changes, the effects of grayscale were mixed: some students said it made apps less appealing, while others found it frustrating for tasks that rely on colour.9 Read more in does grayscale reduce screen time.

5. Use a Focus for work and evenings

A Focus lets you silence notifications, or allow only some, and switch to a chosen Home Screen page while it is on. You can schedule it to start at a set time, place or when you open an app. Set it up in Settings, then Focus.10

A Focus Home Screen page with only calm, useful apps is a simple way to cut phone use without deleting anything.

6. Move tempting apps off your Home Screen

Touch and hold an app, tap Remove App, then tap Remove from Home Screen. The app moves to App Library and still works from there.11 It is not a block. It just stops the app catching your eye every time you unlock.

7. Charge your phone outside the bedroom

In a 2023 survey of 2,005 US adults for the American Academy of Sleep Medicine, 87 percent said they regularly sleep with their phone in their bedroom.12 The NHS suggests trying not to use phones, tablets or computers for an hour before bed.13

Charging in the kitchen or hallway makes that far easier. A basic alarm clock removes a common reason for keeping it by the bed. If it has to stay in the room, set up Downtime on iPhone for bedtime.

8. Use an app blocker for the times you slip

If you keep skipping your own limits, a firmer block can help. In a randomised experiment with about 2,000 US adults on Android phones, a tool for setting app limits that in most cases could not be immediately overridden reduced time on six tempting apps by 22 minutes a day, or 16 percent, over 12 weeks.14

Blockers work best aimed at a clear weak spot, such as late night scrolling or a work block, rather than your whole day.

Reduce screen time: FAQs

How do I see my screen time on iPhone?

On iOS 27, go to Settings, Screen Time, then Daily Average and tap Week or Day. On iOS 26, go to Settings, Screen Time, then See All App & Website Activity.

What is the fastest way to reduce screen time?

Check your Screen Time report, find your top one or two apps, and put a limit or block on just those. Turning off notifications you do not need takes only a few minutes too.

Does grayscale really reduce screen time?

A 2020 study of undergraduate students linked grayscale to 37.9 minutes less screen time a day on average. A later study of pharmacy students found mixed results, so treat it as worth trying rather than guaranteed.

Should I turn off all notifications?

Probably not. In one experiment, people who batched notifications three times a day felt more attentive and in control, while those with no notifications reported more anxiety and fear of missing out.

Why do my app limits not stop me?

On iOS 26, Apple says Screen Time limits can be ignored by default once reached. Set a Screen Time passcode and turn on Block at End of Limit, ideally with someone else holding the passcode.

What is a screen time restrictor?

Any setting or app that limits when or how long you can use apps. Apple Screen Time is built in, and dedicated app blockers add scheduled or firmer blocks.
