How to block websites on iPhone

There are two routes now, one for your own phone and one for a child's, and most guides mix them up. Here is which is which.

By Jacob Redshaw · 14 September 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer

To block websites on iPhone, open Settings, tap Screen Time, then Content and Privacy Restrictions. Turn it on, tap App Store, Media, Web and Games, then Web Content. Choose Limit Adult Websites, then add each specific address you want blocked under the Never Allow list.

You can block websites on iPhone without installing anything. It is built into Screen Time and it is free. The confusion is that Apple now has two different routes, depending on whose phone it is, and most guides online describe one while you are standing in the other.

How to block websites on iPhone, on your own device

This is the one you want if you are doing it to yourself.

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Tap Screen Time.
  3. Tap Content and Privacy Restrictions and turn it on.
  4. Tap App Store, Media, Web and Games.
  5. Tap Web Content.
  6. Select Limit Adult Websites.
  7. Under Never Allow, tap Add Website and type the address you want blocked.1

Repeat the last step for each site. There is no bulk import, so this is as tedious as it sounds, but you usually only need three or four.

Two details worth getting right while you are in there. Type the plain domain rather than a full link with a path, because the restriction applies to the site rather than to one page on it. And do not bother adding both the version with www and the version without, since the filter treats them as the same site. If a block seems not to be working, the cause is almost always a browser rather than a typo, which is covered below.

If you want to go further, the same screen has Allowed Websites Only, which blocks the entire web except a list you build. That is drastic and genuinely effective for a few hours of deep work.

Route two: blocking websites on a child's iPhone

Apple's newer parental controls work differently. You go to Settings, tap Family, tap your child's name, then Apps and Websites. Select a website and tap Block Website, then confirm. Approved and blocked items are organised into Allowed and Blocked lists, and you can unblock at any time.2

This route needs the device to be in a Family Sharing group. It will not appear if you are trying to restrict your own phone, which is the single most common reason people follow a guide and cannot find the menu it describes.

The two routes also behave differently in a way Apple does not spell out. The Never Allow list is a filter you are applying to yourself, and you hold the passcode, so it is only ever as strong as your willingness to leave it alone. The Family route is an approval system held by somebody else, which is why it is genuinely enforceable for a child and completely unavailable as a way of restraining yourself. If you have been looking for a way to lock your own phone down so that you cannot undo it, that option does not exist in Screen Time, and no amount of menu hunting will produce it.

Four things blocking a website does not do

It does not cover the app

Blocking the Instagram website does nothing to the Instagram app, and the reverse is also true. The two are separate systems and you have to do both. A lot of people block the app, feel finished, and then scroll the same service in Safari that evening.

It does not survive another browser

Web Content restrictions are strongest in Safari. Third party browsers are handled inconsistently. If you have Chrome or Firefox installed, test whether your block actually holds in them, and consider deleting them while you are at it.

It does not stop you removing it

Anyone with the Screen Time passcode can undo all of this in about ten seconds. If you set the passcode to something you know by heart, you have built a reminder rather than a block.

Set a random passcode. Write it on paper. Put the paper somewhere that costs you a walk. This one step does more than everything else on this page.

It does not help with timing

A blocked website is blocked all day. That is right for some things and wrong for most. If the real problem is that you lose the hour before bed, you do not want the site gone permanently, you want it gone between 10pm and 7am. For that you need a Screen Time Schedule rather than a content restriction.

Why the free route is worth trying first

Reducing use genuinely works. A 2025 randomised controlled trial in BMC Medicine took 111 students averaging 276 minutes a day and had 58 of them hold under two hours a day for three weeks. Against the control they improved on stress, wellbeing, depressive symptoms and sleep quality.3

The same trial found usage climbing back towards baseline after the limit ended.3 So what matters is not whether you can block a site today. It is whether the arrangement is still standing in three months, which is mostly a question of whether it needs you to maintain it.

Block websites on iphone: FAQs

How do I block a specific website on my iPhone?

Settings, then Screen Time, then Content and Privacy Restrictions. Turn it on, tap App Store, Media, Web and Games, then Web Content. Choose Limit Adult Websites and add the address under Never Allow.

Why can I not find the Web Content setting?

Usually because you are following a guide written for a child's device. Blocking on your own phone uses Content and Privacy Restrictions. Blocking for a child uses Settings, then Family, then their name, then Apps and Websites.

Does blocking a website also block the app?

No. They are separate. Blocking the Instagram website leaves the Instagram app working normally, and blocking the app leaves the website working. You have to do both.

Can I block all websites except a few?

Yes. On the Web Content screen choose Allowed Websites Only and build a short list. It is drastic, but it works well for a couple of hours of focused work.

Do website blocks work in Chrome on iPhone?

Not reliably. Web Content restrictions are strongest in Safari and handled inconsistently elsewhere. Test it in whatever browser you use, and consider deleting the extra browsers.

Can I stop myself unblocking a site?

Only partly. Anyone with the Screen Time passcode can undo it in seconds, so the useful step is using a random passcode you have written down and stored somewhere inconvenient.
