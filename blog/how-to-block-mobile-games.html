How to block mobile games on iPhone

Games are the one category where a recognised disorder exists, and the one where the money matters as much as the time.

By Jacob Redshaw · 14 September 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer

To block mobile games on iPhone, set a Screen Time Schedule and select the Games category rather than individual apps, since games are easy to install and forget. Then restrict in app purchases separately under Content and Privacy Restrictions, because the money and the time are two different problems.

To block mobile games on iPhone you want the Games category rather than a list of apps, and you want to handle the spending separately from the time.

How to block mobile games on iPhone

  1. Open Settings, then Screen Time.
  2. Tap Screen Time Schedule, turn it on, and set the hours.1
  3. Select the Games category rather than picking apps individually. Mobile games are easy to install and easy to forget, and a hand picked list only covers what you thought of today.
  4. Open Always Allowed and make sure nothing game shaped is sitting in it.
  5. Set a Screen Time passcode you have not memorised.

Now block the spending, which is a separate setting

This is the part that gets missed, and for games it often matters more than the hours.

Go to Settings, then Screen Time, then Content and Privacy Restrictions, and turn it on. You can restrict in app purchases from there.2

Mobile game economies are built around small purchases at moments of frustration, which is precisely when your judgement about value is worst. Restricting purchases does not stop you playing. It stops the twenty pounds at eleven at night that you will not be able to account for in the morning.

The one category with a recognised disorder

Games are unusual here. Phone addiction is not a diagnosis anywhere, but the World Health Organization does recognise gaming disorder in ICD-11.

WHO defines it as a pattern of gaming behaviour characterised by impaired control over gaming, increasing priority given to gaming over other activities to the extent that gaming takes precedence over other interests and daily activities, and continuation or escalation of gaming despite the occurrence of negative consequences.3

Two qualifiers always get dropped when this is quoted, and they matter. The pattern must be severe enough to significantly impair personal, family, social, educational or occupational functioning, and it would normally have been evident for at least 12 months.3

Most people who feel they play too much do not meet that bar, and that is worth knowing rather than catastrophising. But if you do read those criteria and recognise yourself, this is a page about settings and you should be talking to a doctor instead.

Why games resist blocking more than feeds

Three reasons, and they are different from the scrolling problem.

Sunk investment. You have a character, a streak, a base, a rank. Stopping does not just end the session, it feels like abandoning something you built, and that is a genuinely different emotion from putting down a feed.

Scheduled obligations. Daily rewards, timed events, energy that refills. A lot of mobile games are designed so that not playing costs you something, which turns a hobby into an appointment.

Other people. If you play with a team or a guild, stopping has a social cost that falls on them. That is a real constraint rather than an excuse, and it is why total deletion is harder here than with a feed.

Given that, scheduled hours tend to work better than deletion for games, which is the opposite of what the evidence suggests for social media.

A setup that works around the obligations

Given sunk investment and timed events, a rigid block tends to get dismantled. Three adjustments that survive better.

Block the night, leave the day. Daily rewards and events are almost always claimable across a wide window, so losing 10pm to 7am rarely costs you anything in the game. It is the cheapest hour to give up and the one doing most of the damage to your sleep.

Tell the people you play with. If a team relies on you, one message setting an expectation costs nothing and removes the social pressure that would otherwise undo the arrangement.

Treat the spending separately and permanently. Purchase restrictions have no in game cost at all, so there is no reason ever to lift them. If you genuinely want to buy something, turn it on deliberately in daylight.

For the general principle behind all three, see how to use your phone less.

What cutting back does

A 2025 randomised controlled trial in BMC Medicine took 111 students averaging 276 minutes of phone a day and had 58 hold under two hours a day for three weeks. Against the control group they improved on stress, wellbeing, depressive symptoms and sleep quality.4

Screen time then returned towards baseline once the limit stopped.4 So a permanent modest arrangement beats a strict fortnight.

One more thing worth checking, because it catches people who think they have finished. If a game is also installed on an iPad, blocking it on your phone moves the behaviour rather than reducing it, and tablet play does not feel like phone play, so the hours are easier to justify. The settings are the same, and the route is in how to block apps on iPad.

Do these two first: restrict in app purchases, and schedule the Games category to be unavailable from an hour before bed. The money one takes thirty seconds.

Block mobile games: FAQs

How do I block games on my iPhone?

Set a Screen Time Schedule and select the Games category rather than individual apps, since games are easy to install and forget. Then restrict in app purchases separately.

How do I stop in app purchases in games?

Settings, then Screen Time, then Content and Privacy Restrictions. Turn it on and restrict purchases there. For games this often matters more than limiting the hours.

Is gaming addiction a real diagnosis?

Yes, unlike phone addiction. The WHO recognises gaming disorder in ICD-11, requiring impaired control, gaming taking priority over other activities, and continuation despite harm.

How long does it have to go on to be a disorder?

The WHO says the pattern would normally have been evident for at least 12 months and must significantly impair personal, family, social, educational or occupational functioning.

Why are games harder to quit than social media?

Sunk investment in a character or rank, scheduled obligations like daily rewards and timed events, and social cost if you play with other people who rely on you.

Should I delete the game or just limit it?

For games, scheduled hours often work better than deletion, because deleting can mean losing progress and letting down teammates. That is the reverse of the advice for feeds.
