How to block social media on iPhone

Blocking the apps is the easy half. The half that defeats most people is everything that is still reachable afterwards.

By Jacob Redshaw · 14 September 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer

To block social media on iPhone, open Settings, tap Screen Time, then Screen Time Schedule and select the Social category rather than individual apps. Then block the matching websites under Content and Privacy Restrictions, because blocking the apps leaves every browser version working normally.

To block social media on iphone you need to do two things, and almost everybody does only the first one and then wonders why nothing changed.

Step one to block social media on iPhone: the apps

  1. Open Settings and tap Screen Time.
  2. Tap Screen Time Schedule. This was called Downtime before iOS 27.
  3. Turn it on and set the hours you want social apps unavailable.
  4. When choosing what to block, select the Social category rather than picking apps one by one. The category catches things you forget and anything you install later.
  5. Add a separate weekend routine if your weekends differ.

Using the category rather than individual apps matters more than it sounds. Hand picking apps means your block covers exactly the services you thought of on the afternoon you set it up, and nothing else. Install something new in March and it is unrestricted.

The category approach has one quirk worth knowing. Apple decides which apps count as Social, and the classification does not always match your intuition. Messaging apps, video apps and some games sit in categories you might not expect, so after selecting Social, open your schedule and confirm the specific services you lose evenings to are genuinely covered. A block working perfectly on apps you never open is the quietest kind of failure.

Step two: block the websites, which is the step people skip

Blocking the Instagram app does nothing to Instagram in Safari. They are separate systems, and the website is a complete substitute.

This is the single most common reason a social media block achieves nothing. You blocked the apps, you kept your word, and you spent the evening on the same services through a browser without it ever feeling like cheating.

  1. Settings, then Screen Time, then Content and Privacy Restrictions. Turn it on.
  2. Tap App Store, Media, Web and Games, then Web Content.
  3. Select Limit Adult Websites.
  4. Under Never Allow, add each site: instagram.com, tiktok.com, x.com, facebook.com, reddit.com, and anything else that applies to you.1

There is no bulk import, so this is tedious, and you only need to do it once. More detail in how to block websites on iPhone.

Type the plain domain rather than a full link with a path, since the restriction applies to the whole site. Do not bother adding both the version with www and the version without, because the filter treats them as the same site. And add the short link domains if you know them, because a shared link that opens in a browser is the usual way people end up back on a site they blocked without having decided to go there.

Three things to check afterwards

Always Allowed. Anything on that list ignores your schedule entirely. Open it and strip it to Phone and Messages. If a browser is in there, your website blocks are pointless. See the Always Allowed trap.

Your other browsers. Web Content restrictions are strongest in Safari and handled inconsistently elsewhere. If you have Chrome or Firefox installed, test whether the block holds, and consider deleting them.

Your passcode. If you can type your Screen Time passcode without thinking, none of the above is really a block. Generate a random one, write it on paper, put the paper somewhere that costs you a walk.

Hours or all day

Hours, almost always.

An all day block is the version people set in a burst of resolve and dismantle within a fortnight, usually for a legitimate reason. Once it is off, it stays off.

Specific hours survive, because nothing legitimate is lost. Start with the hour before bed and the first hour after waking. Those two repeat 365 times a year and have almost nothing defending them: nobody needs Instagram at 11:40pm or at 6:50am.

Or just delete them

Worth saying plainly, because the evidence for deletion is stronger than the evidence for limiting.

A randomised study of 2,844 Facebook users found that four weeks of verified deactivation produced small but significant increases in wellbeing, more offline socialising, reduced political polarisation, and a large persistent drop in use even after the experiment ended.2

Compare that with limiting. In a 2025 randomised trial where 58 students held under two hours a day for three weeks, screen time climbed back towards baseline by the six week follow up.3

One honest caveat from the deactivation study: it also found reduced factual news knowledge.2 If a feed is where you get your news, pick a replacement first.

Delete the app rather than the account. You keep your messages and photos, and the habit is attached to the icon.

If deleting everything feels impossible, delete one thing for two weeks. Not forever, which is too big to agree to and is why people keep postponing it. Two weeks is long enough to break the reflex and short enough that you will actually start, and most people find they do not reinstall, which is information you cannot get any other way.

The two minute version: Screen Time Schedule on the Social category for your worst two hours, the matching websites in Never Allow, and Always Allowed stripped to Phone and Messages.

Block social media on iphone: FAQs

How do I block all social media on my iPhone?

Set a Screen Time Schedule and select the Social category rather than individual apps, then add the matching websites to the Never Allow list under Content and Privacy Restrictions.

Why do my social media blocks not work?

Usually because you blocked the apps but not the websites, or because something is sitting in your Always Allowed list, which ignores your schedule entirely.

Should I block by category or by app?

By category. Hand picking apps means your block only covers what you thought of at the time, and anything you install later is unrestricted.

Is it better to block social media all day?

No. All day blocks get dismantled within a fortnight, usually for a legitimate reason, and then stay off. Specific hours survive because nothing legitimate is lost.

Is deleting social media better than blocking it?

The evidence leans that way. A randomised deactivation study found a lasting drop in use afterwards, while a separate limiting trial saw usage return towards baseline.

Do website blocks work in Chrome?

Not reliably. Web Content restrictions are strongest in Safari and inconsistent elsewhere, so test your block in whatever browser you use and consider deleting the extras.
