How to block Netflix at night and stop binge watching

Nobody decides to watch four episodes. The player decides for you. Here is how to shut streaming down at bedtime on iPhone, and turn off the autoplay that starts it.

By Jacob Redshaw · 14 September 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer

To block Netflix at night on iPhone, add a Screen Time schedule covering your bedtime hours and leave the streaming category out of it. On iOS 26 that is Downtime with Block at Downtime turned on. Then switch off Autoplay Next Episode in your Netflix profile so one episode stops becoming three.

Binge watching rarely starts as a decision. You finish one episode, the next one starts on its own, and the choice you are actually making is whether to reach over and stop something that is already playing. If that is how your evenings end, you can block Netflix at night so the app is closed before the first episode ever begins.

A night block is a rule you set in advance that closes chosen apps for a window of hours around sleep. It works on streaming apps for the same reason it works on feeds: it removes the moment of decision from the moment you are least able to make it.

The design detail that turns one episode into three

Netflix documents a profile setting called Autoplay Next Episode, which sets Netflix to automatically play the next episode in a series.1 It is a profile level setting, so it follows you across devices.

The interesting part is the safety net, and how late it arrives. Netflix says the "Are you still watching?" prompt exists so you do not lose your place or use data when you are not actually watching. On TVs and streaming devices, Netflix says it appears after 3 episodes and 90 minutes of uninterrupted watching. On other devices, it appears after watching 3 episodes of a TV show in a row without using any video player controls.2

So by the design Netflix describes, the app does not check on you until episode four. Three episodes is already most of an evening. That is why relying on the prompt to stop you does not work, and why the useful lever is earlier.

There is a second autoplay too. Netflix documents Autoplay Previews, which plays preview videos while you browse.3 That is the one that keeps you on the home screen for twenty minutes deciding.

Turn off autoplay inside Netflix first

Do this before you set up any blocking, because it costs nothing and it changes the default.

  1. Open the Netflix app and select your profile.
  2. Tap My Netflix, tap your profile name, then tap Manage Profiles.1
  3. Select the profile you want to change.1
  4. Toggle the switch next to Autoplay Next Episode to turn the setting off.1
  5. While you are there, toggle Autoplay Previews off as well.3

Netflix says your preference updates automatically, and that you may need to refresh a device to pick up the change.1 Do it for every profile you actually use.

Honest limit: this changes what the player does. It does not change what you do. Autoplay off makes stopping the default, which is a real improvement, but you can still tap next four times.

How to block Netflix at night on iPhone with Screen Time

Apple's built in tool is free and already on the phone. In iOS 27, Apple describes Schedules as setting when during the day you can use your apps, and Time Allowances as setting how long, with categories including Entertainment.4

  1. Go to the Settings app on your iPhone.
  2. Tap Screen Time, tap Screen Time Schedule, then turn on Schedule.4
  3. Tap Add Schedule, then tap the days of the week you want to cover.4
  4. Tap the Time Window, name it Night, then set start and stop times around your bedtime.4
  5. Choose which Time Allowance categories stay available in that window, and leave Entertainment out of it.4
  6. Check Always Allowed, where Apple lets you choose apps and contacts that work at all times regardless of any schedule or allowance.4

Apple notes that Screen Time Schedules and Time Allowances need iOS 27 or later across your Apple devices.4 A category block covers the other streaming apps at the same time, which is usually what you want. Blocking only Netflix tends to move the problem one icon to the right.

On iOS 26, use Downtime

Go to Settings, tap Screen Time, tap App and Website Activity and turn it on, then tap Downtime and tap Scheduled. Choose Every Day or Customize Days and set your times. Daily limits live under App Limits.5

Apple says that by default, Screen Time limits can be ignored once reached, and that turning on Block at Downtime, which needs a Screen Time passcode, stops restricted apps being opened during downtime. They appear dimmed with an hourglass icon.5 Without that toggle, a block on a streaming app is a suggestion. More in why Screen Time limits stop working.

Match it to a wind down

Apple's Health app lets you schedule bedtimes and wake up times, and says a Sleep Focus helps reduce distractions before and during bedtime, for example by filtering out notifications and phone calls.6 You can set a wind down period from 15 minutes to 3 hours before your bedtime, and Apple says the Sleep Focus begins at the start of that wind down.6

Streaming before bed is a habit with a shape: it fills the gap between finishing the day and being ready to sleep. Giving that gap something else to do matters as much as the block itself.

Why a night block beats a daily streaming limit

A daily limit on a streaming app is a bad fit, because episodes are not minutes. A 55 minute episode does not care that you have 20 minutes left, and being cut off mid episode is the one thing guaranteed to make you turn the limit off.

Block Netflix at night: FAQs

How do I block Netflix at night on my iPhone?

Go to Settings, tap Screen Time, tap Screen Time Schedule, then add a time window over your bedtime and leave Entertainment out of the allowed categories. On iOS 26, use Downtime with Block at Downtime on.

How do I turn off autoplay next episode on Netflix?

Open the Netflix app, tap My Netflix, tap your profile name, tap Manage Profiles, choose a profile, then toggle Autoplay Next Episode off. Netflix says the preference updates automatically.

Why does Netflix only ask if I am still watching after three episodes?

That is how Netflix describes it. On devices other than TVs, the prompt appears after three episodes in a row without using any video player controls. On TVs it is three episodes and 90 minutes uninterrupted.

Should I block all streaming apps or just Netflix?

Block the category. If you block one app, the habit usually moves to the next icon along. Apple's Time Allowance categories, including Entertainment, let you cover them together.

Does blocking Netflix on my iPhone block it on my TV?

No. A Screen Time schedule or an iPhone app blocker covers that iPhone only. For a television, turning off Autoplay Next Episode in your Netflix profile is the setting that helps most.

Is a daily limit better than a night block for streaming?

Usually not. Episodes run longer than the minutes you have left, so a limit cuts you off mid episode and you switch it off. A night block stops the next episode starting instead.
