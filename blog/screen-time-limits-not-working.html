Screen Time limits not working? Why they are easy to ignore, and how to make them stick

You set a limit, the limit arrives, and you tap straight past it. Here is why that happens and what actually makes Apple Screen Time hold.

By Jacob Redshaw · 14 September 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer

If you find your Screen Time limits not working, the usual reason is that they were never set to block. Apple says limits can be ignored by default once reached. On iOS 26 and earlier, turning on Block at End of Limit or Block at Downtime, with a Screen Time passcode someone else sets, makes them much harder to skip.

If your Screen Time limits are not working, you are not imagining it and you are not broken. Apple Screen Time is the iPhone feature that reports how you use your phone and lets you set schedules and time limits for apps.1 Out of the box, those limits act more like reminders than locks.

This guide explains why, which settings make limits stronger, what changed in iOS 27, and when a dedicated app blocker makes more sense.

Why are my Screen Time limits not working?

The short answer: by design, they bend. Apple's own iOS 26 guide says that by default, Screen Time limits can be ignored once reached.2 Nothing is faulty. The limit did its job, which was to tell you.

Downtime works the same way unless you change it. Apple explains that when Block at Downtime is off, you see a reminder that it is downtime, but you can choose to keep using the app.2

Ignore Limit and One More Minute

When an app limit runs out, the screen that appears gives you a way past it. A June 2026 guide from Tech Lockdown describes tapping Ignore Limit to add 15 more minutes or skip the limit for the rest of the day.3 Even with a stricter setup, you can usually take one more minute to finish what you are doing before the passcode is needed.3

At 11pm, tired, with a video half watched, that one tap is very easy to make.

The passcode you already know

A Screen Time passcode must be entered before anyone can change the Screen Time settings you picked.4 That works well for a parent and child. It works far less well when you are both people.

If you set the passcode yourself, you can change or remove it whenever you like.4 Apple also notes that turning off App & Website Activity turns off Downtime, App Limits and Always Allowed in one go.1 So the lock is only as strong as your willpower in the moment you want to open it.

How do you make Screen Time limits stick?

These steps use the iOS 26 names (Downtime, App Limits). If your iPhone is on iOS 27, skip to the next section.

  1. Set a Screen Time passcode. Go to Settings, tap Screen Time, scroll down and tap Lock Screen Time Settings. Enter a four digit code twice.4
  2. Turn on Block at End of Limit. Go to Settings, then Screen Time, then App Limits, and open each limit. The Block at End of Limit toggle only appears once a Screen Time passcode is set.3
  3. Turn on Block at Downtime. Go to Settings, tap Screen Time, tap Downtime, then turn on Block at Downtime. Apple says restricted apps then cannot be opened during downtime and appear dimmed with an hourglass icon.2
  4. Trim Always Allowed. Apps on this list stay available during downtime and after you reach time limits.2 Keep it to calls, messages and anything you genuinely need.
  5. Let someone else hold the passcode. Ask a partner or friend to type it in so you never see it.
Watch the recovery step: Apple lets you reset a forgotten Screen Time passcode with the Apple Account used when Screen Time Passcode Recovery was set up, and that can be a different account from the one on the phone.5 If the other person enters their own Apple Account at that step, you cannot quietly reset it with yours.

What changed with Screen Time in iOS 27?

Apple's iPhone User Guide for iOS 27 describes Screen Time differently. Instead of Downtime and App Limits, it covers a Screen Time Schedule (when apps are available) and Time Allowances (how long you can use categories like Entertainment, Games and Social Media).6 Apple says you need iOS 27, or the matching 27 version of its software, on all your Apple devices to use them.6

  • Schedule: Settings, then Screen Time, then Screen Time Schedule, then turn on Schedule.6
  • Allowances: Settings, then Screen Time, then Time Allowances.6
  • Passcode: Settings, then Screen Time, then Manage Screen Time, then Lock Screen Time Settings.7

At the time of writing (September 2026), Apple's iOS 27 guide explains how to set these up but does not describe what you see when an allowance runs out. So check it on your own phone before you rely on it, and still keep the passcode with someone else.

Can you bypass Screen Time as an adult?

Honestly, yes, if you know the passcode. The passcode is what unlocks the settings, so Screen Time cannot stop the person who holds it. That is not a flaw in you. It is how self set limits work.

Research backs up the idea that firmer limits help. In a randomised experiment with about 2,000 US adults on Android phones, run in 2020, economists gave some people a tool to set app limits that in most cases could not be immediately overridden. The researchers contrast this with the flexible limits in iOS Screen Time. That limit tool reduced time on six tempting apps by 22 minutes a day, or 16 percent, over 12 weeks.8 People valued the option too: 78 percent set binding limits and kept using them.8

When does a dedicated app blocker help?

Apple Screen Time is built into your iPhone, so start there. A dedicated app blocker for iPhone is worth a look when:

  • You keep turning your own limits off, and nobody else can hold the passcode.
  • Your problem has a clear time, like scrolling in bed, rather than total hours.
  • You want the block to start by itself, without setting it up each night.

Screen time limits not working: FAQs

Why does my iPhone let me ignore Screen Time limits?

Apple's iOS 26 guide says Screen Time limits can be ignored by default once reached. To stop that, set a Screen Time passcode and turn on Block at End of Limit for app limits or Block at Downtime for Downtime.

Where is Block at End of Limit on iPhone?

On iOS 26 and earlier, go to Settings, Screen Time, App Limits and open a limit. The toggle only appears after you set a Screen Time passcode.

Can I get rid of One More Minute?

Not completely. Even with Block at End of Limit on, you can usually take one more minute before a passcode is needed. Having someone else hold the passcode stops that minute turning into an hour.

Where did Downtime and App Limits go in iOS 27?

Apple's iOS 27 guide describes Screen Time Schedule and Time Allowances instead. Find them in Settings, then Screen Time.

How do I stop myself bypassing Screen Time?

Ask someone you trust to set the Screen Time passcode, and make sure they use their own Apple Account for passcode recovery. If you cannot do that, a dedicated app blocker that blocks on a schedule may suit you better.

Can I set different Screen Time limits for different days?

Yes. On iOS 26, when you add an app limit you can tap Customize Days and set a different amount of time for specific days, such as weekends.
