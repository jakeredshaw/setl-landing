The best app blockers for iPhone in 2027

Seven blockers, judged on one question: can you get past it when you want to. Ratings checked on the UK App Store on 2 November 2026.

By Jacob Redshaw · 14 September 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer

The best app blockers for iPhone in 2027 are the ones you cannot talk your way past. Opal, one sec, ScreenZen, Jomo, Bloom, Apple Screen Time and SETL all block apps, but they differ on how hard it is to undo a block. Enforcement matters more than features.

Choosing between the best app blockers for iPhone usually comes down to a feature list. That is the wrong list. Every blocker on the App Store can hide an app. The only question that predicts whether you still use it in March is how hard it is to undo a block at 11pm when you want to undo it.

So that is what this is ranked on. Enforcement first, everything else second.

Disclosure: I built one of the seven apps below. I am Jacob Redshaw and SETL is mine. I have listed it last, said what it does not do, and every competitor fact here comes from that app's own App Store listing, checked on the UK store on 2 November 2026. Ratings move. Check them yourself before you buy anything.

What the research says before you pick anything

Cutting your screen time works. A randomised controlled trial published in BMC Medicine in 2025 took 111 students with a baseline of 276 minutes of phone use a day and asked 58 of them to stay under two hours a day for three weeks.1 Against the control group, the intervention group came out better on stress, well being, depressive symptoms and sleep quality, with small to medium effect sizes.

Then the interesting part. Screen time climbed back quickly once the three weeks ended, and by the six week follow up it was approaching where it started.

That is the whole argument for a blocker rather than a detox. The benefit is real and the benefit is rented. Stop paying the friction and you lose it.

Now the uncomfortable half. The one published study I can find that tested Apple's own Downtime and App Limits found nothing. Vu and Tagliabue ran a seven day trial at Oslo Metropolitan University in 2025 and reported no significant evidence that the nudge reduced screen time.2 Be careful with that one: only 17 people finished it, eight of them in the nudge group, so it is a pilot and not a verdict. But it points the same way as every person who has ever tapped Ignore Limit.

How I judged the best app blockers for iPhone

  1. Can you undo it instantly? One tap to unblock is not a blocker, it is a reminder.
  2. Does it run without you? A block you have to start every night is a block you will forget on the night it matters.
  3. Does it cover the day and the night? Most only do one.
  4. What does it know about you? Apple's Screen Time framework hands apps private tokens, so a well built blocker never learns which apps you chose.

The seven

1. Opal

The default recommendation, and fairly. Opal: Screen Time Control from Opal OS Corporation holds 4.6 stars from 14,776 UK ratings, by far the most reviewed app in the category.3 Sessions, scheduled blocks, a focus score, and a Deep Focus mode that is genuinely hard to break. Strengths: polish and track record. Weakness: it is the most expensive mainstream option, and the parts that enforce properly sit behind the top tier.

2. one sec

Not a blocker in the usual sense. one sec from riedel.wtf apps intercepts the app you opened, makes you breathe for a moment, then asks if you still want it. 4.8 stars from 7,133 UK ratings.4 Strength: it works on the reflex rather than the decision, and the friction is pleasant enough that people keep it. Weakness: it is a speed bump by design. If you want the app gone, it will not take it away.

3. ScreenZen

The best free option. ScreenZen from ScreenZen LLC carries 4.8 stars from 7,588 UK ratings.5 Pauses, intention prompts, limits on how many times you can open something. Strength: most of it costs nothing. Weakness: you configure it, which means you can reconfigure it at the exact moment you should not.

4. Jomo

The power user's pick. Jomo has 4.8 stars from 471 UK ratings and goes deep on routines, schedules and blocking rules.6 Strength: if you like control, it gives you more than anyone. Weakness: the setup is work, and a blocker you have not finished setting up blocks nothing.

5. Bloom

Bloom from Find Your Bloom LLC sits at 4.8 stars from 79 UK ratings and leans on coaching and habit framing rather than brute force.7 Strength: kinder, and that suits some people better. Weakness: small review base so far, and persuasion is softer than enforcement.

6. Apple Screen Time

Free, built in, already on your phone, and you should try it first. Set a Downtime window and app limits and see whether that is enough. For a lot of people it is. For the rest, the problem is the Ignore Limit button, and the Oslo pilot above found no significant reduction when it was tested.2 If you have already tried it and drifted, read why an Apple Screen Time alternative usually comes down to enforcement, not features.

7. SETL

SETL Breaks lets you book a night off in advance, for a wedding or a late shift, so the honest exception does not become the habit of quitting. Ending a block early is a deliberate hold, not a tap. What changes in 2027

Apple renamed the Screen Time features in iOS 27. Downtime is now Screen Time Schedule and App Limits are now Time Allowances. Nothing about the behaviour changed, but every guide written before 2027 uses the old names, so if you are following along in Settings and cannot find Downtime, that is why.

Best app blockers for iphone: FAQs

What is the best app blocker for iPhone in 2027?

It depends on how much enforcement you need. Opal is the safest mainstream buy, ScreenZen is the best free option, one sec is the gentlest, and SETL is built for automatic blocking at night and in the day. Try Apple Screen Time first.

Is there a free app blocker for iPhone?

Yes. Apple Screen Time is built in and free, and ScreenZen is free for most of what it does. The catch with free tools is that you can usually switch them off in a second, which is the moment they are meant to help.

Can you bypass an app blocker on iPhone?

Usually yes, with effort. Every blocker built on Apple's Screen Time framework can be removed by deleting the app or changing settings. Good blockers make that slow and deliberate instead of a single tap.

Do app blockers actually reduce screen time?

Reducing screen time has measured benefits for stress, mood and sleep in a 2025 randomised trial. In that trial usage returned to baseline once the limit stopped, which suggests the benefit lasts only as long as the friction does.

Why can I not find Downtime on my iPhone any more?

Apple renamed it in iOS 27. Downtime is now called Screen Time Schedule and App Limits are now Time Allowances. The features work the same way.

Which app blocker is best for ADHD?

The ones that need the least ongoing decision making, since remembering to start a block is the part that fails. Automatic schedules beat manual sessions. SETL and Opal both run on a schedule without being opened.
