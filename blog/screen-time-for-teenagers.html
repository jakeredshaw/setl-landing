Screen time for teenagers

Apple's family controls are a genuinely different tool from a self restriction app, and for a teenager they are the right one.

By Jacob Redshaw · 14 September 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer

On screen time for teenagers, use Apple's family controls rather than a self control app your child can delete. Set them in Settings under Family, where you hold the approvals. iOS 27 added category based daily allowances, age based guidance and separate weekday and weekend schedules.

Screen time for teenagers needs a different tool from the one an adult would choose for themselves, and the distinction is not obvious from the App Store.

A self control app is something you install on your own phone and can remove at any time. That is by design: Apple requires every app to be removable, and blockers run on a permission you granted and can revoke. For a teenager, that makes them close to useless, because the removal takes about ten seconds.

Apple's family controls work the other way. The approvals are held by you, on your device, and your child cannot lift them. That is a genuinely enforceable arrangement and nothing you install can match it.

What Apple offers for screen time for teenagers

Set it up in Settings, then Family, then your child's name.1

The relevant parts, with the iOS 27 names:

  • Time Allowances. A daily time budget spanning all categories, which you can then fine tune by adjusting the time allotted to any individual category.2 This replaced the older per app limits.
  • Screen Time Schedules. These let you choose which apps your child can access at different times of day, with separate routines for weekdays and weekends.2 Genuinely useful for a school week.
  • Apps and Websites. Approve or block individual apps and sites, organised into Allowed and Blocked lists, and you can unblock at any time.1
  • Content restrictions and purchase restrictions. Including in app purchases, which for games matters as much as the hours.
  • Age based guidance, and settings that sync across your child's devices.2

Apple says all the latest Screen Time features require iOS 27, iPadOS 27 or later,2 so an older device in the household will not show everything.

One thing Apple's tools will not do

They will not stop a determined teenager entirely, and it is better to know that at the start.

Workarounds circulate, devices get borrowed, and a browser on a games console or a friend's phone is outside anything you have configured. Treating the settings as a sealed system sets you up for a confrontation when it turns out not to be one.

What they do well is remove the frictionless default. A restricted phone makes the easy path the one you set, which handles the great majority of ordinary use without needing enforcement. That is a worthwhile and realistic goal, and it is a different goal from making it impossible.

Three things worth getting right

Block every device, not just the phone. An iPad with no restrictions makes a restricted iPhone irrelevant, and tablet use does not feel like phone use, so the hours are easier to justify. The settings are the same, and the route is in how to block apps on iPad.

Cover the websites too. Blocking an app leaves the browser version working, which is the most common way a careful setup achieves nothing.

Use the weekday and weekend split. A single bedtime applied to all seven nights is wrong on Friday, and the usual outcome is that it gets switched off on Friday and never switched back on.

The part that is not a settings problem

I am a founder of an app blocker, not a parenting expert, so I will keep this short and stick to what I can support.

Two things are worth saying. A rule the adults in a house do not follow themselves does not work, and that is not a moral point: children calibrate against behaviour rather than instruction. And a restriction imposed without explanation tends to be experienced as a punishment and attacked as one, while the same restriction explained is at least arguable.

On the evidence side, be sceptical of anything you are told confidently about teenagers and screens. Most of the data is observational, which means it cannot separate whether screens cause difficulty or whether struggling teenagers use screens more. The randomised trials in this area are mostly on adults and students. A 2025 trial in BMC Medicine found modest improvements in stress, wellbeing, depression and sleep when students cut to under two hours a day, with effects described as small to medium.3 That was 111 people with a mean age of 22, not a study of teenagers.

If your child's phone use is genuinely affecting their sleep, mood or school, that is worth raising with a GP rather than solving with settings alone. Depression, anxiety and ADHD all produce heavy phone use and all are treatable.

A practical note on the passcode, since it is the part that quietly decides everything. Use one your child has not watched you type, change it if they have, and do not use a birthday or a house number. Shoulder surfing is how most of these arrangements end, long before anybody looks up a workaround online.

Start here: Settings, then Family, then your child's name. Set a Screen Time Schedule with a separate weekend routine, cover every device they own, and restrict in app purchases.

Screen time for teenagers: FAQs

What is the best screen time app for teenagers?

Apple's own family controls, set in Settings under Family. A self control app can be deleted by your child in seconds, which makes it unsuitable as a parental control.

Why can my teenager delete an app blocker?

Because Apple requires every third party app to be removable, and blockers run on a Screen Time permission that can be revoked. Family controls work differently, with approvals held by you.

How do I set up parental controls on iPhone?

Settings, then Family, then your child's name. From there you set Time Allowances, Screen Time Schedules, app and website approvals, and purchase restrictions.

What changed in iOS 27 for parents?

Allowances became a single daily budget you can fine tune per category, schedules can choose which apps are available at different times, and weekday and weekend routines can differ.

Should I block my teenager's iPad too?

Yes. An unrestricted iPad makes a restricted iPhone irrelevant, and tablet use does not feel like phone use, so the hours are easier for everyone to justify.

Is screen time harmful for teenagers?

Be careful with confident claims. Most data is observational and cannot separate cause from effect, and the randomised trials are largely on adults and students rather than teenagers.
