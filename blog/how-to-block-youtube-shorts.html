How to block YouTube Shorts on iPhone

The honest answer first: iOS cannot block one feature inside an app. Here is what actually reduces Shorts, in order of how well it works.

By Jacob Redshaw · 14 September 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer

You cannot block YouTube Shorts on their own using iPhone Screen Time, because it blocks whole apps rather than features inside them. The options are blocking YouTube entirely at set hours, using YouTube in Safari where you can avoid the Shorts shelf, or deleting the app.

If you want to block youtube shorts and keep normal YouTube, the honest answer is that iPhone cannot do that, and no third party app can either.

Here is why, and then what actually helps.

Why you cannot block YouTube Shorts on their own

Apple's Screen Time framework works at the level of apps and categories. It can make YouTube unavailable. It has no way to reach inside YouTube and disable one section of it.

Third party blockers are built on the same framework, so they inherit exactly the same limit. Any app claiming to block Shorts specifically is either describing something else or is not being straight with you.

That is a design decision rather than an oversight. Apple gives blockers opaque tokens representing the apps you chose, deliberately, so the blocker never learns which apps they are. The privacy protection and the feature limitation are the same mechanism.

It is worth noticing that the only party who could switch Shorts off cleanly is YouTube, and YouTube has no commercial reason to build that switch. Short form is where the engagement is. Any setting they offer is likely to be a reminder or a time nudge rather than a genuine removal, which puts the whole problem back on your side of the line.

What actually works, in order

1. Block YouTube at set hours

Not all day. Specific hours, usually the evening, decided in advance.

This is the strongest option because it is enforced and it requires nothing of you in the moment. Set a Screen Time Schedule covering the hours you lose, and make sure YouTube is not in your Always Allowed list.

Yes, you lose the long videos too during those hours. That is the trade, and for most people the hours they lose to Shorts are the hours they would not have watched a documentary in anyway.

2. Use YouTube in Safari instead of the app

Delete the app and use the website. The web version behaves differently and is generally less aggressive about pushing short form content at you, and critically it is less comfortable, which is the point.

Making something slightly worse to use is a legitimate strategy. You will watch the thing you actually wanted and then stop, because there is less pulling you onwards.

3. Set a Time Allowance on the Entertainment category

A budget rather than a block. When it runs out you are told, and you can continue, which is the weakness. But it does give you a ceiling and it will tell you how much Shorts is genuinely costing you, which most people underestimate by a lot.

4. Clear your watch history and stop engaging

Recommendation systems respond to what you watch. If your feed is mostly Shorts, that is partly a reflection of what you have watched, and it will adjust if your behaviour changes. Slow, partial, and genuinely does something over weeks.

5. Delete the app entirely for two weeks

The nuclear option and the one with the best supporting evidence. A randomised study of 2,844 Facebook users found that four weeks of verified deactivation produced small but significant wellbeing gains and a large persistent drop in use even after the experiment ended.1

Removal appears to change the relationship with a thing. Limiting appears to pause it.

Why short form is harder than ordinary video

Worth understanding, because it explains why you can watch one film and stop, and cannot watch one Short and stop.

A film has an end. When it finishes you are returned to a decision, and at that point you usually go to bed.

A short form feed never finishes. Each item is seconds long, so you never reach a natural boundary, and the next one begins before you have evaluated the last. There is no moment where continuing becomes a choice, which means you do not decide to watch for forty minutes. You simply never arrive at the point where you would have stopped.

That is the same structural problem as any infinite feed, compressed. It is not a weakness in your character and no amount of resolve addresses it, because resolve operates on decisions and the format removes the decisions.

The compression also makes the time harder to notice. Forty minutes of film is one memory. Forty minutes of Shorts is around a hundred items, none of which you will be able to describe tomorrow, which is why people consistently guess low when asked how long they spent. If you want to be unsettled by something accurate, check the figure in Settings rather than trusting your sense of it.

What the evidence says about cutting back

A 2025 randomised controlled trial in BMC Medicine took 111 students averaging 276 minutes of phone a day and had 58 hold under two hours a day for three weeks. Against the control group they improved on stress, wellbeing, depressive symptoms and sleep quality.2

Screen time then returned towards baseline once the limit ended.2 So make whatever you choose permanent rather than a fortnight's effort.

Shortest advice: you cannot block Shorts alone, so block YouTube for the hours you keep losing and use the website for everything else.

Block youtube shorts: FAQs

Can you block YouTube Shorts without blocking YouTube?

Not on iPhone. Screen Time works at the level of whole apps and categories, so it cannot disable one section inside an app. Third party blockers use the same framework and have the same limit.

How do I stop seeing YouTube Shorts?

The practical options are blocking YouTube for set hours, using the website instead of the app, setting a budget on the Entertainment category, or deleting the app for a couple of weeks.

Does using YouTube in Safari help?

Often yes. The web version is less comfortable to use and less aggressive about pushing short form content, and making something slightly worse to use is a legitimate strategy.

Why is short form video so hard to stop watching?

Because each item is seconds long and the next starts before you have evaluated the last, so you never reach a natural boundary where continuing becomes a decision.

Will clearing my watch history reduce Shorts?

Partly and slowly. Recommendations respond to what you watch, so behaviour change does shift the feed over weeks, but it is a weak lever compared with blocking the app at set hours.

Is deleting the app better than limiting it?

The evidence leans that way. A randomised deactivation study found a lasting drop in use afterwards, while a separate limiting trial saw usage return to baseline once the limit stopped.
