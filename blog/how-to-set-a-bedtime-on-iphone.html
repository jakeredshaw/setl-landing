How to set a bedtime on iPhone

Your iPhone has two separate bedtime features and neither works properly on its own. Here is how to combine them.

By Jacob Redshaw · 14 September 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer

To set a bedtime on iPhone, use Sleep Focus in the Health app for your sleep schedule, then add a Screen Time Schedule covering the same hours. Sleep Focus dims the screen and silences notifications. Only Screen Time actually makes apps unavailable.

There are two ways to set a bedtime on iphone and they do completely different things. Most people set up one, notice nothing much changes, and conclude the phone cannot help.

You need both, and you need them covering the same hours.

The two features and what each actually does

Sleep Focus

Lives in the Health app under Sleep, and appears as a Focus mode. It sets your sleep schedule, dims the Lock Screen, silences notifications and can wake you with an alarm.

What it does not do is stop you using anything. Sleep Focus will happily dim the screen while you scroll for another hour. It is a quietening feature, not a blocking one.

Screen Time Schedule

Lives in Settings under Screen Time. This is the one that makes your chosen apps unavailable during hours you set. In iOS 27 it is called Screen Time Schedule; it was called Downtime before that. Apple's guide tells you to tap Screen Time, then tap Screen Time Schedule or Time Allowances.1

Setting Sleep Focus without this is the single most common mistake. You get a dim screen and no restriction, which is close to the worst combination, since the notifications are silenced but the feeds are open.

How to set a bedtime on iPhone, both parts

Part one: Sleep Focus

  1. Open the Health app.
  2. Tap Browse, then Sleep.
  3. Set your Sleep Schedule, with bedtime and wake time.
  4. Set different weekday and weekend schedules if yours differ.
  5. Turn on Sleep Focus so notifications are silenced during those hours.

Part two: Screen Time Schedule

  1. Open Settings, then Screen Time.
  2. Tap Screen Time Schedule and turn it on.
  3. Set it to start 30 minutes before your Sleep Focus bedtime.
  4. Set it to end an hour after your wake time, to protect the morning as well.
  5. Add a separate weekend routine, which iOS 27 supports.2
  6. Open Always Allowed and strip it to Phone and Messages. Anything else on that list ignores the schedule completely.
  7. Set a Screen Time passcode you have not memorised.

What time to set it for

Start the block 30 minutes before you want to be asleep, not at the moment you want to be asleep.

This is the detail that decides whether it works. A block that begins at your target sleep time arrives after the decision has already been made and lost. You are in bed, holding the phone, mid scroll. Thirty minutes earlier catches you while you are still deciding whether to go upstairs.

For the weekend, be generous. If your weekday block starts at 22:00, make Friday and Saturday 00:30. A schedule you disable on Friday is a schedule you do not re enable on Saturday, and then it is gone.

Why bother with the phone side at all

Because this is where the sleep goes. The American Academy of Sleep Medicine surveyed 2,007 US adults in 2025 and found most were prioritising screen time over sleep at bedtime.3

And cutting back measurably helps. A 2025 randomised controlled trial in BMC Medicine had 58 students hold under two hours of phone a day for three weeks, and sleep quality on the Insomnia Severity Index improved against the control group, with a mean difference of 2.59.4

Note the intervention there. Not a dimmer screen, not a warmer colour temperature. Less phone.

That is worth dwelling on, because the colour temperature features get far more attention than they deserve. Night Shift and a dimmed Lock Screen are both free and there is no reason to turn them off, but neither addresses the reason you are awake, which is that you are still using the phone. A warmer screen at half past midnight is a better version of a bad situation.

The other reason to set the Screen Time side properly is that bedtime is the hour with the least to defend it. Nobody needs a feed at 11:40pm. There is no work justification, no family obligation and no urgency. Every other hour of your day has something legitimate mixed in, which makes blocking it a genuine trade off. This one does not, which is why it is the cheapest hour you will ever buy back and the sensible place to start.

Three things that will undo it

Your phone is your alarm. Then you handle it last thing and first thing, and no schedule survives that. Buy a ten pound alarm clock and charge the phone in another room.

Safari is in Always Allowed. An allowed browser gives you every blocked site. Very common.

You know the passcode. Then the bedtime is a suggestion that costs four seconds to decline.

The minimum viable version: Screen Time Schedule from 30 minutes before bed to an hour after waking, Always Allowed down to Phone and Messages, and an alarm clock so the phone leaves the room.

Set a bedtime on iphone: FAQs

How do I set a bedtime on my iPhone?

Use two features together. Set your Sleep Schedule and Sleep Focus in the Health app, then add a Screen Time Schedule in Settings covering the same hours, starting 30 minutes earlier.

Does Sleep Focus block apps?

No. Sleep Focus dims the Lock Screen and silences notifications, but you can still open everything. Only a Screen Time Schedule makes apps unavailable.

What time should my block start?

Thirty minutes before you want to be asleep. A block starting at your target sleep time arrives after you are already in bed holding the phone, which is too late to help.

Should the bedtime be different at the weekend?

Yes, and make the weekend window generous. iOS 27 supports separate weekday and weekend routines, and a schedule you disable on Friday is usually one you never switch back on.

Why does my iPhone bedtime not stop me using my phone?

Most likely you set Sleep Focus but not a Screen Time Schedule, or apps are sitting in your Always Allowed list, which exempts them from blocks entirely.

Should my phone stay in the bedroom?

Preferably not. If the phone is your alarm you will handle it last thing and first thing, which undermines any schedule. A cheap alarm clock and a charger in another room fixes it.
