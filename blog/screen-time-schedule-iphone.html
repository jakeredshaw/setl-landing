Screen Time

Screen Time Schedule on iPhone, set up properly

Screen Time Schedule is the setting that used to be Downtime. It is the strongest free thing on your iPhone, and almost everyone leaves one switch wrong.

By Jacob Redshaw · 14 September 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer

Screen Time Schedule on iPhone replaced Downtime in iOS 27. It makes your chosen apps unavailable during hours you set in advance, and it supports separate routines for weekdays and weekends. Set it up in Settings, then Screen Time, then Screen Time Schedule, and remember to check your Always Allowed list afterwards.

Screen Time Schedule is the most useful free feature on your iPhone and the one with the worst name recognition, because Apple renamed it in iOS 27. It used to be called Downtime. Same place in Settings, new label.

Apple's support guide now tells you to tap Screen Time, then tap Screen Time Schedule or Time Allowances.1

It is worth more than the allowance feature sitting next to it, for one reason. An allowance waits until you have already spent the time. A schedule decides in advance, which means it is enforced by the version of you who set it up rather than the version of you at midnight.

How to set up a Screen Time Schedule

  1. Open Settings and tap Screen Time.
  2. Tap Screen Time Schedule.
  3. Turn it on.
  4. Set the start time. Make it 30 minutes before you want to be asleep, not at the moment you want to be asleep. The point is to remove the apps before the decision arrives, not during it.
  5. Set the end time for whenever you want them back.
  6. Set a separate weekend routine if your Friday and Saturday look nothing like your Tuesday.
  7. Now do the step everyone skips, below.

The switch that cancels the whole thing

Go back and open Always Allowed.

Everything in that list ignores your schedule completely. The default contents are reasonable, Phone and Messages and so on, but most people add things during setup, forget, and then conclude the feature is broken when the app they actually lose evenings to opens fine at 1am.

Strip it back to what you would genuinely need in an emergency at 3am. Phone. Messages, if someone might need you. Your alarm, if it is a separate app. Nothing else. Not the browser. Not your email.

Then set a Screen Time passcode, and make it one you do not know by heart. If you can type it without thinking, you have built a speed bump rather than a schedule. Generate something random, write it down, and put the paper somewhere that costs you a walk.

Weekday and weekend routines, which is the actual upgrade

The genuinely new thing in iOS 27 is that schedules support separate routines for weekdays and weekends, and let you choose which apps are accessible at different times of day.2

That matters because a single schedule applied to all seven nights is wrong twice. Set it for 10:30pm and it is wrong on Friday, so you switch it off on Friday, and then you do not switch it back on. Set it for 1am so it survives the weekend and it does nothing on the five nights that decide how your week goes.

Two routines solves a problem that was quietly killing people's schedules. If you set up nothing else from this page, set up this.

The weekend window being generous is not a weakness. A schedule you never disable beats a strict one you abandon in week two.

Notice that the weekday window also covers the morning. That half is usually ignored and it is quietly valuable, because the first twenty minutes after waking sets the tone for how reactive the rest of the day feels. Blocking until seven costs you nothing you will miss and removes the habit of starting the day inside somebody else's feed.

Why schedules work better than limits

Reducing phone use has measured benefits. A 2025 randomised controlled trial in BMC Medicine took 111 students with a baseline of 276 minutes a day and asked 58 of them to hold under two hours for three weeks. Against the control group they improved on stress, wellbeing, depressive symptoms and sleep quality.3

The same trial found screen time climbing back to roughly where it started once the three weeks ended. So the thing that matters is not the three good weeks. It is whether the arrangement is still running in March.

A schedule survives better than a limit for a boring reason: it does not need you. It happens at the same time whether or not you remembered, whether or not you are motivated, whether or not today was bad. A limit requires you to respect it at the exact moment you least want to.

Where schedules still fall short

Two places, and you should know both before deciding this is enough.

First, you can turn it off. Settings, Screen Time, off. If you know the passcode, that is about eight seconds of work.

Second, there is no good way to plan an exception. A wedding, a night shift, a flight. Your only option is to disable the schedule, and that is the step where habits die, because switching it back on is an admin task with no deadline attached and nobody checking.

Screen time schedule: FAQs

What is Screen Time Schedule on iPhone?

It is the iOS 27 replacement for Downtime. It makes your chosen apps unavailable during hours you set in advance, and it supports different routines on weekdays and weekends. Find it in Settings, then Screen Time.

How do I set a Screen Time Schedule?

Settings, then Screen Time, then Screen Time Schedule. Turn it on, set a start and end time, add a separate weekend routine, then check Always Allowed and set a passcode you do not know by heart.

Why do apps still open during my Screen Time Schedule?

Almost always because they are in your Always Allowed list, which ignores the schedule entirely. Open that list and strip it back to what you would genuinely need at 3am.

Can I have a different Screen Time Schedule at the weekend?

Yes. iOS 27 supports separate weekday and weekend routines. Make the weekend window late enough that you never switch the whole thing off, because that is how most schedules end.

Is Screen Time Schedule better than Time Allowances?

For a specific problem hour, yes. A schedule decides in advance, so it is enforced by the version of you who set it up. An allowance waits until the time is already spent, then asks you to stop.

Can I stop myself turning off Screen Time Schedule?

Not completely. Anyone with the passcode can switch it off in seconds. Using a random passcode you have written down and stored somewhere inconvenient is the best you can do with the built in tools.
