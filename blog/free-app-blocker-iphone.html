Free app blocker for iPhone

You almost certainly do not need to pay for this yet. Start with the one on your phone, set up the way most people never set it up.

By Jacob Redshaw · 14 September 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer

The best free app blocker for iPhone is Apple Screen Time, which is already installed. Set a Screen Time Schedule, empty your Always Allowed list, and use a passcode you do not know by heart. ScreenZen is the strongest free third party option.

If you want a free app blocker for your iPhone, the honest answer is that you already own the best one and have probably never configured it properly. That is not a trick to get you onto a paid page. It is genuinely where you should start, and for a lot of people it is where this ends.

Option one: Apple Screen Time, free and already installed

Apple renamed these settings in iOS 27, which is why older guides send you to menus that no longer exist. Downtime is now Screen Time Schedule and App Limits are now Time Allowances. Apple's support guide tells you to tap Screen Time, then tap Screen Time Schedule or Time Allowances.1

Set it up like this, in this order.

  1. Settings, then Screen Time, then Screen Time Schedule. Turn it on. Set it to start 30 minutes before you want to be asleep.
  2. Add a separate weekend routine, which iOS 27 now supports.2 Make the weekend window generous, because a schedule you switch off on Friday is a schedule you never switch back on.
  3. Open Always Allowed and strip it to what you would genuinely need at 3am. Phone. Messages. Nothing else. This is the step almost everybody skips, and anything left in that list ignores your schedule completely.
  4. Set a Screen Time passcode. Make it random, write it on paper, and put the paper somewhere that costs you a walk.

Step four is what separates a real free blocker from a decorative one. If you can type the passcode without thinking, you have built a speed bump.

What the free version genuinely cannot do

Three things, and they are the honest limits rather than marketing.

You can turn it off. Settings, Screen Time, off. About eight seconds if you know the passcode.

You can tap past an allowance. When a Time Allowance runs out you are told, and then you may carry on. That is deliberate on Apple's part and reasonable for a feature shipped to everyone.

There is no way to plan an exception. A wedding, a night shift, a flight. Your only option is to switch the schedule off, and switching it back on is an admin task with no deadline and nobody checking. That is where most people's arrangement quietly ends.

Option two: a free app blocker from the App Store

Several blockers are free to download from the App Store, which is not the same as being entirely free. Most carry in app purchases, so check what sits behind the paywall before you commit to one.

On the UK App Store on 2 November 2026, ScreenZen from ScreenZen LLC was free to download and held 4.8 stars from 7,588 ratings.3 It is the strongest free reputation in the category and it works mainly by adding pauses and intention prompts before an app opens.

one sec from riedel.wtf apps was also free to download, with 4.8 stars from 7,133 ratings.4 It intercepts the app you opened, makes you wait a moment, then asks if you still want it.

Both are worth trying. Both are configurable, which is their strength and their weakness: a tool you can reconfigure can be reconfigured at exactly the moment you should not be allowed to.

Worth knowing what free costs elsewhere. An app that reduces how much you use your phone cannot easily be funded by how much you use your phone, so free tools in this category are usually funded by a paid tier, and occasionally by data. Read the privacy label on the App Store listing before you install, and prefer anything that works without an account, since an app with no login has nothing to leak. Free things that are not apps and work anyway

  1. Charge your phone in another room. Buy an alarm clock. This costs about £10 once and does more than most software.
  2. Delete one app for two weeks. Not forever. Two weeks. Most people do not reinstall it, which tells you something you could not have learned by intending to use it less.
  3. Turn off all non human notifications. Keep messages and calls from people. Kill everything from a company.
  4. Log out after every session. Having to type a password converts a reflex into a decision.
Two week test: do the Screen Time setup above and the charger move, and give it a fortnight. If it holds, you have solved this for nothing. If you have tapped past it most days, you have learned something useful about which tool you actually need.

When paying is worth it

One case, really. When you have already done the free version properly and you keep undoing it.

That is not a character flaw, it is information. The evidence suggests the benefit of reducing phone use is real but conditional. A 2025 randomised controlled trial in BMC Medicine had 58 students hold under two hours a day for three weeks and found improvements in stress, wellbeing, depressive symptoms and sleep quality against a control group. Screen time then climbed back towards baseline once the limit ended.5

So the thing you are actually buying, if you buy anything, is not a feature. It is whether the arrangement is still standing in March. Paid tools earn their money by making the undo slow and by running without needing you to remember.

Free app blocker: FAQs

What is the best free app blocker for iPhone?

Apple Screen Time, because it is already installed and enforces at the system level. Set a Screen Time Schedule, empty your Always Allowed list, and use a passcode you have written down rather than memorised.

Is there a completely free third party app blocker?

Several are free to download, which is not the same thing. ScreenZen and one sec were both free to download on the UK App Store in November 2026, but check what sits behind in app purchases before committing.

Why does my free app blocker not work?

Usually the Always Allowed list, which overrides your schedule, or a Screen Time passcode you know by heart. Fix those two things before concluding you need to pay for something.

Can I block apps on iPhone without downloading anything?

Yes. Screen Time is built into iOS and needs no download. In iOS 27 look for Screen Time Schedule and Time Allowances rather than the older Downtime and App Limits names.

Is a paid app blocker actually better?

Only in one specific case: you have set up the free version properly and keep undoing it. What you are buying is slower undoing and automation, not more features.

What free change makes the biggest difference?

Charging your phone in another room and buying a cheap alarm clock. It costs about ten pounds once and beats most software, because it puts physical distance between you and the habit.
