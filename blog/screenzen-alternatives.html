ScreenZen alternatives

ScreenZen is the best free option in the category. The thing that makes it good is also the thing that eventually undoes it.

By Jacob Redshaw · 14 September 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer

The best ScreenZen alternatives depend on why it stopped holding. one sec is gentler and simpler, Opal is the most established paid option, Jomo offers more control, and a scheduled blocker suits you if you keep reconfiguring ScreenZen at the wrong moment.

People search for screenzen alternatives having usually had a good experience that stopped working, which is a different situation from disliking an app.

Credit where it is due first. On the UK App Store on 2 November 2026, ScreenZen from ScreenZen LLC was free to download and held 4.8 stars from 7,588 ratings.1 That is an outstanding rating on a serious number of reviews, for something that costs nothing.

Why ScreenZen stops working for people

Almost always the same reason, and it is not a defect. It is configurable.

ScreenZen works through pauses, intention prompts and limits on how often you can open something. All of those are things you set, which means all of them are things you can change.

And the moment you most want to change them is the moment they are doing their job. At 11:40pm, the shortest path to the app is not deleting ScreenZen. It is opening ScreenZen and adjusting a number. That takes fifteen seconds, feels like a reasonable administrative decision, and leaves you with a tool that technically still works and no longer stops anything.

If that is the pattern you recognise, more configuration will not help. What you need is something where the settings are decided in advance and the undoing is slower than the urge.

This is the central trade off in the whole category and almost nobody states it. Configurability is what makes a tool fit your life, and it is also the escape hatch. The more precisely you can tune something, the more precisely you can untune it at the exact moment tuning it down is the thing you want most. Any app that hands you the dials is trusting the midnight version of you with them.

Which is also why Apple's version, for all its weaknesses, has one structural advantage people overlook. Because the controls live in Settings behind a passcode rather than inside the blocking app, you can genuinely separate yourself from them. Give the passcode to someone else and the dials are gone. No third party app can offer that, because the app itself is always reachable.

ScreenZen alternatives worth considering

If you want something simpler: one sec

Fewer settings by design, which for some people is the entire fix. one sec from riedel.wtf apps was free to download on 2 November 2026 with 4.8 stars from 7,133 UK ratings.2 It intercepts the app, makes you pause, then asks if you still want it. Less to fiddle with means less to undermine.

If you want the most established paid option: Opal

Opal: Screen Time Control from Opal OS Corporation held 4.6 stars from 14,776 UK ratings on 2 November 2026, far more than anything else in the category.3 Sessions, schedules, and a harder focus mode. The safe choice, and the most expensive mainstream one.

If you actually want more control: Jomo

If your complaint about ScreenZen is that it does not go far enough rather than that it goes too far, Jomo is the one. It held 4.8 stars from 471 UK ratings on 2 November 2026 and is the deepest on routines and rules.4

If your problem is a specific hour: SETL

Mine, so weigh it accordingly. SETL blocks at your bedtime automatically rather than asking you to configure thresholds, and ending a block early is a deliberate press and hold rather than a settings change. No reviews yet, having launched in late October 2026, so buy Opal if a track record matters.

If you have not set up the free built in option properly: Apple Screen Time

Different kind of free. Apple's blocking is enforced at the system level rather than by an app, so it cannot be adjusted from inside the app you are trying to avoid. Set a Screen Time Schedule, strip your Always Allowed list, and use a passcode you have not memorised.

The free versus paid question, honestly

An app whose purpose is to reduce how much you use your phone cannot easily be funded by how much you use your phone. So free tools in this category are funded some other way, usually a paid tier, and you should read the privacy label on any of them before installing.

That said, free is the right starting point and ScreenZen is a genuinely good one. Paying only makes sense once you have established that the free version works and you keep overriding it.

Evidence worth keeping in view: a 2025 randomised controlled trial in BMC Medicine found real improvements in stress, wellbeing, depressive symptoms and sleep when 58 students held under two hours a day for three weeks, then found screen time returning towards baseline once the arrangement stopped.5 The tool that wins is the one still running in six months, not the one with the most features.

Screenzen alternatives: FAQs

What is the best alternative to ScreenZen?

Depends on the problem. one sec if ScreenZen had too many settings, Opal if you want the most established paid option, and a scheduled blocker if you keep adjusting your own thresholds at night.

Why does ScreenZen stop working after a while?

Because it is configurable, and the quickest route to a blocked app at midnight is adjusting a setting rather than deleting the app. That takes fifteen seconds and feels like an administrative decision.

Is ScreenZen really free?

It was free to download on the UK App Store in November 2026 with a 4.8 star rating from 7,588 reviews. As with any free app in this category, read the privacy label before installing.

Is Apple Screen Time better than ScreenZen?

Different. Apple enforces at system level, so it cannot be adjusted from inside the app you are avoiding, but it is easier to switch off entirely if you know the passcode.

Should I pay for a blocker if ScreenZen is free?

Only once you have established that the free version works and that you keep overriding it. What you pay for is slower undoing and automation, not more features.

Which blocker has the best ratings?

On the UK store in November 2026, ScreenZen, one sec and Jomo were all around 4.8 stars, while Opal had 4.6 from by far the largest number of reviews at 14,776.
