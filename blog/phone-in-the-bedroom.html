Should you keep your phone in the bedroom?

The cheapest sleep improvement available to most people costs about ten pounds and takes one evening to set up.

By Jacob Redshaw · 14 September 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer

Keeping your phone in the bedroom harms sleep two ways: it delays when you actually go to sleep, and it makes waking in the night more disruptive. Charging it in another room and buying a cheap alarm clock is the single most effective change available.

Keeping your phone in the bedroom is close to universal and it is the easiest sleep problem to fix, because the fix is physical rather than behavioural. You are not being asked to resist anything. You are being asked to move a charger.

What a phone in the bedroom actually costs you

You go to sleep later than you intended

This is the big one and it has nothing to do with light. You meant to sleep at 11. It is now 12:40. The phone was within reach, so the decision to stop had to be made actively, repeatedly, by the most tired version of you there is.

The behaviour is close to standard. The American Academy of Sleep Medicine surveyed 2,007 US adults in 2025 and found most were prioritising screen time over sleep at bedtime.1

And cutting it back measurably helps. A 2025 randomised controlled trial in BMC Medicine had 58 students hold under two hours of phone use a day for three weeks. Sleep quality on the Insomnia Severity Index improved against the control group, with a mean difference of 2.59.2

Night waking becomes worse

Everybody surfaces briefly in the night. That is normal and you usually do not remember it.

If the phone is on the bedside table, those brief surfacings become sessions. You check the time, there is a notification, and twenty minutes later you are properly awake in a way you would not have been. A phone turns a normal non event into an actual period of wakefulness.

And a third, smaller one: light

Worth including honestly rather than leading with. Chang and colleagues found in PNAS in 2015 that evening use of a light emitting eReader reduced melatonin, delayed circadian timing by more than an hour, lengthened the time taken to fall asleep and reduced next morning alertness compared with a printed book.3

Real, but keep the scale in mind. It was 12 adults reading for four hours in a sleep laboratory, and there is a published critique arguing the lighting may have overstated real world effects.4 It is not the main reason your phone is costing you sleep.

The fix, and it is genuinely this simple

  1. Buy an alarm clock. About ten pounds. This is the step people skip, and then they keep the phone in the room because it is their alarm, and nothing changes.
  2. Move the charger to another room. Kitchen, hallway, anywhere that is not where you sleep.
  3. Do it tonight rather than planning to. It takes two minutes and there is nothing to decide.

What makes this work is that it is not a rule. A rule has to be followed, every night, by someone who is tired. A phone in a different room is just a fact about the room. There is nothing to comply with.

If you genuinely cannot move it out

Some reasons are real. You are on call. An elderly relative might ring. You live somewhere where you want it reachable. Those are not excuses and the answer is not to pretend otherwise.

In that case, make the phone present but useless.

  • Put it across the room, not within arm's reach. A phone you have to stand up for is a different object.
  • Block your chosen apps at a fixed time automatically. Calls still come through. The feeds do not. Apple's Screen Time Schedule does this for free.
  • Empty your Always Allowed list down to Phone and Messages. Anything else in there ignores the schedule completely, which is the usual reason people think this does not work.
  • Face down, notifications off. Keep calls from humans, kill everything from a company.

That combination gets you most of the benefit while keeping the one function you actually need at 3am.

One thing not to do, which gets recommended constantly: do not simply switch the phone to silent and leave it beside you. Silent removes the sound and leaves the object, and the object is most of the problem. You will still check it, because checking is not triggered by a notification so much as by a gap in your attention, and a gap in your attention is precisely what lying in the dark produces.

What it will not do

It will not fix insomnia. If you lie awake for an hour with no phone in the room, wake repeatedly, or feel exhausted after a full night, that is worth a doctor rather than a charger relocation. The phone is one input among several and it happens to be the cheapest one to change.

It also will not help much if you are using the phone at night to avoid something. Lying in the dark means being alone with whatever you have been not thinking about all day, and for a lot of people that is exactly what the scrolling is for. No change to the room solves that.

Tonight: move the charger out, set an alarm clock, and if the phone has to stay, put it across the room with the feeds blocked from 30 minutes before bedtime.

Phone in the bedroom: FAQs

Should I keep my phone out of my bedroom?

If you can, yes. It is the cheapest and most reliable sleep improvement available to most people, because it removes the decision entirely rather than asking you to resist the phone every night.

Why is having a phone in the bedroom bad for sleep?

Two main reasons. You go to sleep later than you intended, and normal brief night wakings turn into proper periods of wakefulness because you check the phone.

What should I use as an alarm if not my phone?

A cheap standalone alarm clock, around ten pounds. This is the step people skip, and skipping it is why the phone stays in the room and nothing changes.

Is it the blue light that is the problem?

Mostly not. Light has a measurable effect, but the famous study used 12 adults reading for four hours in a lab. Going to bed ninety minutes late costs you far more.

What if I need my phone nearby for emergencies?

Put it across the room rather than within arm's reach, block your feeds automatically at a fixed time, and keep calls and messages allowed. You get most of the benefit and keep the function you need.

Will moving my phone fix my insomnia?

No. If you lie awake with no phone in the room, wake repeatedly, or feel exhausted after a full night, that is worth seeing a doctor about rather than treating as a phone problem.
